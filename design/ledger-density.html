Each row says it once.

The ledger window printed its verdict three times per row and its evidence twice, then repeated the totals from the status bar in a header that explained what a ledger is. This is what was cut and what stayed.

01

The verdict was printed three times

A roll‑up mark in the leading column, the state word in every destination cell, and the reason under each of those. The mark is arithmetic over the cells beside it — rollUp in status.ts — and the reader can do it by eye when both cells are on screen. It went; the marks moved into the cells, where the state they colour actually lives.

02

Eleven rows, twenty‑two evidence lines, four sentences

Every destination cell carried its own reason. On the archive in the screenshot that meant “source changed since deep verify” nine times and “source changed since last check” seven more. A fact printed sixteen times is not sixteen facts.

The first fix collapsed them into one column per row. That was still wrong: a reason is prose about why no conclusion could be drawn, it barely changes down the page, and every word of it is already on the folder record — per destination, in full, one double‑click away. A column that repeats itself is not made into a column by moving it. The reasons went.

What stayed is the count. “Behind” spans a folder missing one file and a folder missing five hundred, and a number is the one thing the reader cannot arrive at on their own. It is taken from nFiles/nChanges, not by parsing the sentence.

Lost on purpose: the date under a verified row. The word already carries it — the engine turns verified into unverified · deep verify expired the moment the record ages out of its window, so “verified” cannot mean “verified a year ago”. The exact timestamp is on the folder record's Evidence tab, where a timestamp belongs.
03

The header explained a ledger to the person who keeps it

“What the last checks established” tells a reader who reads rsync itemize strings nothing they did not know. “12 folders · 431.38 gb” was a folder count the status bar was already printing six inches away, and a total with nothing to compare it against.

The name moved to the title bar, where macOS puts a window's subject and it costs no content height. The measure moved to the status bar and picked up the thing the reader actually came for: how much of the archive is safe to delete. Bytes, not folders — the TUI footer made the same call, for the same reason.

04

Two circles that would not say what they were

A tick in a circle and a magnifier in a circle, side by side. The tooltip named them, but a tooltip is the answer to a question you have to already know to ask — and neither said that clicking runs the check over all twelve folders.

The labels are forced on regardless of the toolbar's display mode. The split menu now spells out both scopes, so it documents its own primary action instead of hiding it behind a click, and the per‑folder item names the folder rather than saying “selected”.

05

An unplugged drive is one fact, not twelve

Cutting the reasons exposed a gap that was already there. When a destination is not mounted, every row's cell for it reads unchecked — twelve rows stating one fact about one drive, and the ledger never said which fact. The tray panel and Settings both say “not connected”; the window the reader is actually looking at did not.

It belongs to the destination, so it goes under the destination's name, once.

06

One field, two names

The cells read differenceSummary ?? reason. In engine-snapshot.ts the engine emits differenceSummary: cell.reason — the same string, under two keys, with a fallback between them that can never fire. The ledger now reads reason, which is what the roll‑up reads, so the two cannot drift.

Left in place: the engine still emits both, and the folder record still prefers differenceSummary. Worth a follow‑up — one of the two names should go, and that is a change to the protocol, not to this window.
Kept deliberately: the state word in every destination cell, rather than the TUI's bare glyph. A terminal row is 110 cells and buys a legend with the space it saves; a 1040pt window is not short of room, and a legend is a lookup table the reader has to hold in their head. The mark carries the colour, the word carries the meaning without it — which is the rule Theme.swift already states.

The test this ledger now passes: every string on a row is either a measurement or a state word, and no string appears twice. Prose about why a conclusion could not be drawn lives on the folder record, which exists precisely to hold it.
